# Billing and monetization plan — not live

**Reviewed 25 September 2026.** This is an implementation plan, not a live checkout, merchant account, legal opinion, or promise of revenue. Duekrio is currently a free, local-first prototype. There is no payment form, subscription, customer account, premium entitlement, or payment processor connected to the site. No marketing or sales campaign has started. Do not accept card details in a Duekrio form, email, chat, CSV, or note.

## Commercial offer to validate

Keep the present single-device app and fictional demo free. Its current source is [MIT licensed](../LICENSE), so do not pretend that a paywall around the existing bundle creates an exclusive product. Test a **$29/month single-operator Pro** offer only after a distinct, working premium benefit is shipped and demonstrated. The first candidate is a reliable hosted backup and restore service with authenticated access, encrypted storage, clear recovery behavior, and independently tested data isolation. This is a **product hypothesis**, not a shipped capability or a price commitment. It would add server-side personal-data and security obligations. If research shows that buyers value a different capability more, change the offer before billing anyone.

A **$59/month team plan** is a later hypothesis, contingent on real team accounts, permissions, shared state, audit history, and safe synchronization. The present owner field is only a local text label; it is not a team account. A paid guided setup service is not the recommended initial offer: it needs a human to deliver it, and both candidate merchants restrict services. Charge only for a deliverable that already works, describe exactly what the buyer receives, and display the renewal, cancellation, and refund terms before checkout. No collection-rate or time-savings claim should be used without evidence.

The original [first-customer plan](GO_TO_MARKET.md) remains a validation gate: observe repeat use and ask buyers for a paid commitment at an explicit price. A working checkout alone is not proof of demand or profit.

## Provider choice and eligibility

**First route to investigate for the requested payout preference: Lemon Squeezy MoR paying to a verified PayPal account.** It publishes [5% + US$0.50 and no monthly charge](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing) and supports SaaS subscriptions. Its [payout rules](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/getting-paid) allow a verified PayPal account, with automatic twice-monthly payouts above a US$50 threshold; later withdrawal from PayPal depends on that account's actual country agreement. Lemon Squeezy's [prohibited-products list](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/prohibited-products) explicitly mentions regulated *collections* services and generally excludes services/consulting. Duekrio is designed as a B2B invoice-workflow tool, not a debt-collection agency or payment service, but only the provider can decide whether to approve this actual product. Obtain its category decision before relying on the route. [Store activation](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/activate-your-store) requires business information, identity verification, and provider review. Its base transaction fee can have additions for some methods, international transactions, or payouts.

**Alternative MoR: Paddle.** [Paddle's published price](https://www.paddle.com/pricing) is 5% + US$0.50 per Checkout transaction, with no setup or monthly fee on its pay-as-you-go plan. Its [supplier-country policy](https://www.paddle.com/help/legal/sanctions/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle) accepts software businesses outside its excluded-country list; this does **not** establish the owner's actual country or guarantee approval. Paddle's [acceptable-use policy](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle) welcomes B2B SaaS but excludes regulated financial and payment services, credit advice, and other categories. Its [account verification](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/account-verification/what-is-account-verification) requires domain, business (where applicable), and identity checks. Its [domain review](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/account-verification/what-is-domain-verification) requires a live HTTPS site, real pricing and deliverables, terms, refund and privacy policies, and a real operator name. [Identity verification](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/account-verification/what-is-identity-verification) may require government ID, proof of address, and a liveness check. An assistant cannot truthfully invent or bypass those checks. Paddle pays monthly and [does not allow withdrawal on demand](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/when-and-how-do-i-get-paid), so it does not meet that specific preference.

**Direct-merchant alternative: PayPal Business.** [PayPal supports subscriptions](https://developer.paypal.com/platforms/subscriptions/) and can receive sale proceeds into a business-account balance. That balance and available withdrawals depend on the verified account country; PayPal may place holds, reserves, and limits. Unlike a merchant of record, direct PayPal leaves transaction-tax calculation/remittance, refunds, disputes, and chargeback exposure with the owner; see the [illustrative Israel agreement](https://www.paypal.com/il/legalhub/paypal/useragreement-full?locale.x=en_IL). This may fit withdrawal timing better in an eligible jurisdiction, but it is not a shortcut around business identity, tax, or customer protection duties.

**Do not assume Stripe is available.** [Stripe's direct merchant-country list](https://stripe.com/global) does not include Israel as of this review. Stripe may be an option if the real legal business is in a supported jurisdiction and passes its own onboarding; a Hebrew interface, timezone, or computer location does not prove the business jurisdiction. Do not create a foreign entity merely to bypass eligibility without independent legal and tax advice.

## Where sale proceeds can sit

The owner prefers to leave proceeds with a provider and withdraw them later. **Do not build a Duekrio wallet or promise that a processor balance is an insured bank account or escrow.** The payment provider, not this app, must receive card details and hold any balance under its own agreement. No provider account, payout destination, or owner jurisdiction has been verified here.

| Route | When money moves | Fit for owner-chosen withdrawal | Material tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Paddle MoR](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/when-and-how-do-i-get-paid) | Automatic monthly payout once its configured threshold is met. | **No:** Paddle explicitly says sellers cannot withdraw on demand. | Merchant-of-record services reduce some sales-tax/payment operations, subject to approval and fees. |
| [Lemon Squeezy MoR](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/getting-paid) → verified PayPal | Lemon Squeezy creates payouts twice monthly after its hold period and minimum threshold; an eligible verified PayPal account may then retain the received balance. | **Conditional:** withdrawal is from PayPal after its automatic payout, according to the actual account country's agreement. | Requires approval of this product category, owner identity, and a verified payout account. PayPal can limit, hold, or reverse funds. |
| [Direct PayPal Business subscriptions](https://developer.paypal.com/platforms/subscriptions/) | Buyer payments enter the seller's PayPal account, subject to holds and the account-country rules. | **Conditional:** some PayPal agreements allow the owner to choose when to withdraw; verify the actual country and account terms. | The owner is the direct merchant, with transaction-tax, refund, dispute, and chargeback duties. |
| [Stripe manual payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts#payout-schedule) | Configurable only for eligible accounts/countries. | **Conditional:** not offered in every country and not an indefinite-storage promise. | Requires a supported real business jurisdiction and merchant approval. |

For example, [PayPal's Israel agreement](https://www.paypal.com/il/legalhub/paypal/useragreement-full?locale.x=en_IL) describes retaining a balance and requesting withdrawal, but also permits verification, holds, limitations, and reversals; its balance is an unsecured claim, not a deposit. **This example does not establish where the owner operates or what agreement applies.** The closest lower-burden hypothesis is an approved merchant of record paying to an eligible verified PayPal account. If owner-controlled withdrawal timing is essential, direct PayPal may fit better but materially increases the owner's merchant and tax responsibilities. Neither route guarantees funds can remain untouched indefinitely or be withdrawn instantly. Decide only after the real owner, business country, provider eligibility, payout terms, and product category have been verified.

The merchant of record handles checkout card collection and the customer-facing transaction, including applicable customer sales-tax calculation and remittance under its terms ([Paddle's MoR explanation](https://developer.paddle.com/get-started/how-paddle-works/)). That is **not** personal immunity from lawsuits, product/privacy duties, business registration, or tax on the seller's income. [Paddle's payout statements guidance](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/what-statements-will-i-receive) explicitly distinguishes seller income/corporation tax from customer sales tax. Determine actual operator, jurisdiction, banking, and tax position before receiving money.

## Unit economics: scenario, not forecast

For a hypothetical US$29 monthly base price, Paddle's published 5% + US$0.50 fee is US$1.95 per successful transaction. These are simple arithmetic examples **before** other possible fees, currency conversion, refunds, disputes, income tax, hosting beyond free limits, support, or customer acquisition. The amount available to the seller depends on the checkout's tax-inclusive/exclusive setting and actual buyer mix.

## Secure implementation boundary

1. **Before any checkout:** the free [duekrio.pages.dev](https://duekrio.pages.dev/) release is live on Cloudflare Pages; its point-in-time checks are recorded in [HOSTING.md](HOSTING.md). No site has been approved by a payment provider. Complete the real operator/contact and brand review, ship a genuine paid capability, publish accurate pricing, renewal/cancellation/refund terms, and update privacy/terms for billing data and the provider. Keep the existing free data export usable. No paid plan is promised until those steps are complete.
2. **Checkout:** use the approved provider's hosted or isolated checkout origin. Keep provider JavaScript away from the main invoice-storage origin where possible: a third-party script executing on that origin could read default or older plaintext `localStorage` data and data displayed in an unlocked encrypted workspace. For Paddle, the public [client-side token and price ID](https://developer.paddle.com/paddle-js/about/) may be in a billing page, but merchant API keys and webhook secrets must never be bundled into Vite assets or committed. An approved [default payment-link page](https://developer.paddle.com/build/transactions/default-payment-link/) is required for Paddle live checkout. The browser must never collect or forward PAN, CVV, or other card data. Review the exact checkout method, approved origin, Content Security Policy, and privacy notice before enabling it.
3. **Identity and entitlement:** first authenticate a buyer and bind one provider customer/subscription to that account. Choose and review the authentication method and recovery process before implementation; the current app has no accounts. A payment-success URL or client callback is display information only. Grant or revoke premium access only from a verified provider event or a server-to-provider check. Define behavior for renewals, cancellations at period end, failed payments, refunds, chargebacks, replay, and temporary provider outages. Give customers a route to access and export their own data after cancellation.
4. **Webhook backend:** a future server endpoint (for example, a separately built Cloudflare Worker or Pages Function) must verify the provider signature against the **raw** body using a server-held secret. [Paddle uses `Paddle-Signature` with a time window](https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/about/signature-verification/); [Lemon Squeezy signs `X-Signature` with HMAC-SHA256](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/webhooks/signing-requests). The current Pages deployment is static and has no such endpoint. Cap request size and reject unsupported methods. Create checkout server-side for an authenticated account and a fixed approved price; store the provider checkout/transaction ID mapped to that account. Do not grant access from a success URL or from client-supplied webhook `custom_data` alone. Store event IDs for idempotency, account/subscription state, and an event timestamp or provider readback so duplicated or out-of-order deliveries cannot re-enable a canceled plan. Both providers may retry failed events ([Paddle](https://developer.paddle.com/webhooks/about/how-webhooks-work/), [Lemon Squeezy](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/webhooks/webhook-requests)). Redact billing PII from logs, rotate secrets, and test forged, replayed, duplicated, delayed, failed-payment, refund, and cancellation events before live use.
5. **Data separation and encryption:** keep cardholder data entirely with the MoR. If premium cloud backup is built, design tenant authorization and key management first; encrypt transport and stored content, specify who can decrypt, and test recovery and deletion. The current free app offers optional passphrase-encrypted local saves; default and older browser `localStorage` copies remain readable until the user opts in and explicitly removes any older copy after a verified encrypted backup. An unlocked tab can still expose decrypted data. CSV exports and plain JSON backups remain **unencrypted**. An optional passphrase-encrypted JSON backup protects that downloaded file; losing its passphrase means the file cannot be restored. A cipher added without key recovery, access control, and backup restore tests is not a security solution. A local-only client-side paywall is also bypassable, especially when the current source is public under MIT. Server-side entitlements must protect an actual server-delivered premium service.

There is intentionally no live buy button, real price ID, provider API key, account, or charge in this repository. Build and sandbox-test the integration when the account, approved domain, real product offer, and operator identity exist. Then verify an end-to-end test purchase, renewal, cancellation, failed payment, refund, payout reporting, and the actual live site before accepting real payments. No architecture can guarantee that fraud, security breaches, claims, or chargebacks are impossible.
