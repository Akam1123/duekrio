**Title: How to turn an AR aging report into a weekly action list**

An accounts receivable aging report tells you which invoices are open and how long they have been outstanding. It rarely tells you *why* a particular invoice is still unpaid or who should resolve the issue. A weekly review becomes useful when each exception ends with one owner and one next action.

Start with a current export from your accounting system. Check that the file contains customer, invoice number, **remaining balance**, and due date. If the report includes both original invoice amount and remaining balance, use the remaining balance for follow-up. Keep the report date visible: a CSV is a snapshot, and an invoice could have changed since export.

Sort the open items into three working groups:

1. **Waiting on the customer:** They have the invoice and need to confirm a date or approve payment.
2. **Blocked by information:** A purchase order, timesheet, tax form, or corrected invoice is missing.
3. **Disputed:** The customer questions the scope, amount, or service. Resolve the dispute before another generic reminder.

For each invoice, write the smallest useful next step. “Follow up” is vague; “send revised PO reference to AP by Thursday” is a task someone can finish. Record one responsible person and a review date. If the customer promises to pay on a particular date, store the date and the source of the promise in a note. A promise is a commitment to check, not confirmed cash.

Consider a fictional example: invoice `INV-1042` has a $2,400 remaining balance and is 35 days overdue. The buyer's AP team says its PO number is missing. The owner is Maya, and the next action is “send corrected invoice with PO 8174 today; confirm receipt Friday.” A generic “Your invoice is overdue” email would miss the real blocker. The action list gives the team a way to finish the specific job.

Before sending a follow-up, compare the invoice with the current ledger and recent customer correspondence. Keep the email factual and short: invoice reference, current amount and due date, what you believe is blocking payment, and the one answer you need. A human should check the recipient, balance, and tone. Do not treat an exported board as the source of truth for paid status.

At the next weekly review, close completed actions, deliberately reschedule unfinished ones, and reconcile the list with a fresh accounting export. If an invoice disappears from the new file, investigate rather than assuming it was paid. If it remains after you marked it paid locally, verify the ledger before contacting the customer.

### Post 2 — a practical checklist

Before the next overdue-invoice meeting, try this five-column list:

1. Remaining balance and due date
2. Reason payment is stuck
3. Person responsible for the next step
4. Specific next action and date
5. Customer promise, if one actually exists

The useful change is replacing “chase customer” with a task like “send corrected PO reference by Thursday.” Check balances and payment status in your accounting system before contacting anyone.
